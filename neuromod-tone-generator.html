Classic mode uses the fixed frequency ratios from the original acoustic coordinated reset (CR) method (Tass et al., 2012): tones at 0.766×, 0.9×, 1.1×, and 1.4× the center frequency. Auditory frequency discrimination is roughly logarithmic, so ratio-based spacing keeps tones perceptually equidistant regardless of how high or low the center frequency is. Spectral analysis of a reference sample confirmed these exact ratios (frequencies matched to within 0.3% at a 4000 Hz center).

Custom mode generalizes this to any number of tones using log-spaced ratios: each step multiplies by (1 + spacing%).

Burst structure: what looks like slow amplitude modulation in a spectrogram is actually a rapid sequence of short pulses. Each pulse is a brief tone at one of the ratio-spaced frequencies above, chosen independently at random each time (repeats allowed — not a fixed shuffle). Pulses run back-to-back on one continuous oscillator that steps frequency and dips to a partial low point between them (the "inter-pulse dip" — not full silence), then a full pause follows once the burst finishes. All of this was reverse-engineered from a reference recording: pulse duration ~160ms, ~13 pulses per burst, dip to ~32% of peak between pulses, consecutive-repeat rate ~23% (close to the ~25% expected from pure random selection over 4 tones), and ~700ms silence between bursts.
